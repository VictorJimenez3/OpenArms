# OpenArms — Accessibility & Market Validation

HackPrinceton research handoff · November 14, 2026

## Executive findings

**OpenArms has a plausible use case when a student wants to direct a real experiment, a specific physical step still requires help, and the course can assess the student's scientific decisions separately from manual technique.** Published work establishes that these barriers occur and that several accommodations can preserve student control. Whether OpenArms would improve on those accommodations, fit a teaching lab's workflow, or attract a buyer remains open. [Jeannis et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30729844/) · [Esteb et al., 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [University College Cork, 2022](https://www.ucc.ie/en/sefs/news/2022/robots-will-help-students-with-disabilities-complete-lab-classes.html)

- **The loss can be access, practice, or control.** In a purposively recruited US survey of 107 current and former postsecondary students with physical disabilities, about a quarter reported a barrier at the lab entrance, about half reported being limited in their lab roles, and 74% found peers helpful in completing lab tasks. Both peer help and restricted participation occurred in the sample. These self-reports describe respondents across science and engineering and cannot estimate how common a specific unmet need is at a given campus. [Published survey](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30729844/) · [Study author’s dissertation and item-level results](https://d-scholarship.pitt.edu/33864/1/HJ_ETD_Final_Apr2018.pdf)
- **Direct control already exists in several forms.** A Butler undergraduate directed an assistant through two organic chemistry labs. A chemical engineering student in Uruguay operated adapted instruments herself across her degree. University College Cork adapted experiments for four biochemistry classes to run on a computer-controlled liquid handler expressly for accessibility. The first two document individual outcomes. Cork documents the purchase and adaptation work, with student outcomes and sustained use unverified. [Butler case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [Gonzalez Muzio, Moyna and Rocca, 2026, full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408490932_Adaptation_of_laboratory_equipment_for_use_by_students_with_disabilities) · [Cork biochemistry project](https://www.ucc.ie/en/sefs/news/2022/robots-will-help-students-with-disabilities-complete-lab-classes.html)
- **The reason to use real equipment depends on what students need to learn.** A 35-study meta-analysis found no statistically significant overall difference in conceptual learning between physical and virtual inquiry. Most studies were in physics and few assessed outcomes beyond conceptual knowledge. A student's ability to work with actual measurement variation, recover from an unexpected result, or demonstrate a manual technique requires its own assessment. [Muilwijk and Lazonder, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1163024)

**Scope and evidence.** This is the accessibility and market-validation workstream from the shared OpenArms conversation. The project combines accessible student controls, a physical robotic arm and educational simulation. The shared analysis and delegation discussion supplied the project context. This targeted review focused on postsecondary teaching labs, with clearly identified examples from undergraduate research, graduate work and engineering where they clarify a specific barrier or alternative. The 2026 scoping review found 20 eligible studies describing 129 technologies and adaptations, with much of the digital work focused on visual disabilities. That count describes the literature, and the studies below vary in what they establish. This report is desk research. OpenArms interviews, usability data, cost data and purchase commitments remain outstanding. [Original OpenArms conversation](https://chatgpt.com/share/6af80b60-1a80-83e9-84f2-90c34e524f89) · [Mesquita et al., 2026, abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10554181261456999)

## 1. Documented barriers and workarounds

| Lab task or condition | What the student experienced | Workaround and what it preserved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reach into a fume hood, carry materials and assemble apparatus in organic chemistry.** | A Butler undergraduate with limited reach, grip and strength struggled to reach the working area inside the hood. His usual way of carrying objects against his body was unsuitable for hazardous materials. | He could move empty glassware and perform simpler steps. A trained assistant handled other steps under his direction, including selecting and positioning glassware, clamps and hoses. The student reported that specifying each action helped him understand the labs. The authors report completion of every experiment across two semesters. [Esteb et al., single case with student feedback](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) |
| **Dispense fixed and variable volumes, operate stopcocks, transfer and weigh solids, and run titrations.** | Standard analytical chemistry equipment required upper-limb movements that chemical engineering student Agustina Rocca could not perform with the original controls. | The Universidad de la República team developed chin-actuated controls, motorized burette operation and other instrument-specific aids. Rocca co-designed and tested the adaptations. The authors report she independently completed her required practicals and met the usual accuracy and assessment criteria. This is one documented degree-long outcome. [Gonzalez Muzio, Moyna and Rocca, 2026](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408490932_Adaptation_of_laboratory_equipment_for_use_by_students_with_disabilities) |
| **Pipette, pour, carry items and fasten a lab coat in life-science wet labs.** | Monica Mesecar describes how cerebral palsy affects finger dexterity and endurance, making these tasks difficult. She also describes difficulty finding undergraduate research that was both accessible and relevant to her interests. Her detailed accommodation account spans research placements and post-baccalaureate work. | She used or requested ergonomic and repeater or multichannel pipettes, plastic labware and a coat with easier fasteners. Lab members or a volunteer helped with tasks she still found cumbersome, under her direction. Height adjustment and unobstructed aisles addressed separate access needs. [Mesecar’s first-person account](https://stemwithdisabilities.squarespace.com/stories/monica-mesecar) |
| **Steady a pipette, open microcentrifuge tubes repeatedly and grip bottles.** | Graduate student Katharine Hubert describes hand joints that fatigue quickly and need support during wet-lab work. | A bench-mounted armrest helps stabilize her arm for pipetting, a tube opener reduces repetitive thumb motion, and grip tape helps her open bottles and hold items. She reports that these aids help her work independently. [Hubert’s first-person account](https://stemwithdisabilities.squarespace.com/stories/katharine-hubert) |
| **Load a microscope slide, position the specimen and see through the eyepieces.** | In a Purdue prototype evaluation, five wheelchair or scooter users described difficulty loading slides and positioning the stage of a conventional microscope. Four of those five identified eyepiece positioning as a major barrier. | Seven undergraduate and graduate participants with mobility impairments completed common microscopy tasks with a motorized microscope, a monitor, a prefilled slide loader and individually selected computer inputs. They received verbal directions. The evaluation demonstrates task access under those conditions. [Duerstock, AccessScope evaluation](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~bsd/papers/duerstock_2006.html) · [Journal article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16796240/) |
| **Physically handle reaction vessels and take an active role in chemistry practicals.** | Chemist Lukas Guggolz describes his undergraduate experience with very limited limb movement. He mostly observed peers, and on two occasions directed a peer through the lab work himself. | Faculty arranged full-time lab attendance and equivalent-workload assignments when the usual practical was infeasible. He completed the degree. His account shows that observation, direction and alternative assessment offered different forms of participation within the same program. [Guggolz, first-person editorial, 2023](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.inorgchem.3c01276) |
| **Sustain a long practical or make up one missed during a flare.** | Chemistry student Victoria Ogden describes missing an organic chemistry lab and being unable to arrange a make-up. She tried to catch up in a six-hour session without breaks, experienced a flare and dropped the lab course. | Ogden valued flexible make-up or equivalent assessment options in other coursework. A separate RSC case describes a doctoral student with tremors and mobility challenges who used a lean stool for breaks and paid help for loading NMR rotors while continuing independent bench work. [Ogden, first-person account, p. 30](https://openlibrary-repo.ecampusontario.ca/jspui/bitstream/123456789/1689/1/Making-Lab-Based-Courses-Inclusive-1669839520.pdf#page=36) · [RSC NMR case](https://www.rsc.org/policy-and-campaigning/science-culture/disability-inclusive-laboratories-in-the-chemical-sciences/case-studies-library/adjustments-for-student-with-tremors-and-mobility-challenges) |

These examples distinguish **access to the room and workstation**, **ability to execute a step**, and **ownership of the experiment**. A student may retain ownership while directing someone else's hands, and a student at a powered instrument may still need someone to load samples. Evidence is strongest for concrete task barriers and individual accommodations. How frequently any particular step remains inaccessible after accommodation is unknown. [Jeannis et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30729844/) · [Butler case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [AccessScope evaluation](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~bsd/papers/duerstock_2006.html)

The educational significance of a manipulation depends on the assignment. Dispensing a volume can be a means to test a hypothesis about concentration, or it can be the skill being assessed. Likewise, choosing a specimen and interpreting an image can be assessed with an accessible microscope, while preparing a slide is a separate task. An access solution can work for one of these objectives while leaving another unmet. [ACS laboratory skills](https://www.acs.org/education/policies/acs-approval-program/guidelines/laboratory.html) · [ASM microbiology skills](https://asm.org/guideline/asm-curriculum-guidelines-for-undergraduate-microb) · [AccessScope evaluation](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~bsd/papers/duerstock_2006.html)

## 2. Existing accommodations and where they fall short

| Approach | What it already does well | Remaining constraints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accessible workstation and adapted hand tools** | Adjustable benches, reachable controls, nonslip mats, clamps, handled containers and powered fillers can enable students to work at the bench with peers and directly operate tools. Electronic pipettes and tube openers can reduce thumb force or repetition. | An electronic pipette still requires positioning, handling tips and usually handling tubes. A lower bench can leave a deep hood or an instrument control out of reach. Fit depends on the student, equipment and room. [University of Washington DO-IT](https://doit.uw.edu/knowledge-base/what-are-examples-of-science-lab-accommodations-for-students-with-mobility-disabilities) · [Alyssa Paparella’s first-person account](https://ncas.ac.uk/towards-more-accessible-laboratory-practices-with-alyssa-paparella/) |
| **Student-directed assistant or structured peer support** | A trained assistant can fetch supplies, manipulate materials and respond to unusual setups while the student specifies actions, observes and interprets. UConn hires assistants for physical tasks such as lifting and pouring, with the student directing their work. | Staffing, training, schedule coverage and role clarity matter. A peer can be valuable for collaboration, and unstructured pairing can leave the student watching or taking notes. An assistant also cannot supply personal practice when that specific manipulation is being assessed. [UConn assistant policy](https://disabilities.center.uconn.edu/accommodations/accommodation-types/lab-and-discussion-assistants/) · [ACS directed-assistant guidance, §3.9.2](https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/about/governance/committees/cwd/teaching-chemistry-to-students-with-disabilities-5th-edition.pdf) · [Jeannis et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30729844/) |
| **Instrument-specific controls and automation** | A motorized microscope, adapted burette or automated liquid handler can give direct control over a well-defined action. The Opentrons OT-2, for example, has documented pipetting capabilities and education materials for serial dilution and molecular biology. | Each tool serves a narrower workflow and may require custom fixtures, labware or setup. Someone still owns preparation, calibration and cleaning. Published vendor capabilities do not establish compatibility with a student's assistive technology or a specific course's assessment. [AccessScope](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~bsd/papers/duerstock_2006.html) · [Opentrons education](https://opentrons.com/education) · [OT-2 maintenance](https://docs.opentrons.com/ot-2/maintenance/cleaning/) |
| **Remote access to real instruments or a live lab partner** | The Open University provides remote access to physical instruments, including a scanning electron microscope. In a biomedical engineering course, LabMate let remote students talk with an in-room partner and troubleshoot in real time. Five focus-group participants preferred it to prior online lab delivery. | A remotely operated instrument is bounded by its controls and preloaded materials. Live partner access depends on the partner and connection. LabMate documented setup burden, audio and video problems, and lacked direct achievement data on course objectives. Remote participation also needs deliberate access to instructors and classmates. [OpenSTEM Labs](https://stem.open.ac.uk/study/openstem-labs/about-us) · [LabMate study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9851733/) |
| **Virtual labs, flexible make-ups and home kits** | ChemCollective offers free simulated aqueous chemistry for practice and make-ups. Labster documents keyboard navigation, built-in reading and resizable text in supported simulations. Flexible options can give students time to repeat or resume work. | Simulations can assess concepts and modeled decisions. A home kit still requires handling tools and materials, and a simulation's access features vary by activity. A simulation alone cannot show how the student responds to actual equipment or demonstrate physical technique. [ChemCollective instructor guide](https://chemcollective.org/teachers/teacherGuide) · [Labster accessibility-mode guide](https://help.labster.com/en/articles/3095203-open-and-play-labster-simulations-in-accessibility-mode) · [ACS laboratory guidance](https://www.acs.org/education/policies/acs-approval-program/guidelines/laboratory.html) |

A Canadian mixed-methods study of 58 students, six of whom identified as disabled, illustrates the format tradeoff. Students valued the pace and flexibility of online labs and the technical practice, collaboration and access to academic support in person. The results describe perceptions across the sample. The 2026 remote-lab study cited in the shared chat adds evidence about scheduling and instructional support. Its small mobility subgroup leaves preferences for motor-access controls unresolved. [Takkouch, Zukowski and Campbell, 2023](https://jpaap.ac.uk/JPAAP/article/view/581) · [Remote laboratory needs study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/pds.2026.10654)

## 3. Differentiation and counterevidence

- **Closest accessibility precedent — University College Cork.** The team bought an OT-2 through a college digital-inclusion initiative and funded a biotechnology student to adapt experiments for four biochemistry classes. The stated goal matches OpenArms closely: preserve reagent choices, calculations, planning and students' own results while delegating pipetting. The announcement verifies an institutional purchase for a specific project. Continued use, students' experiences and measured learning outcomes remain open. [University College Cork project announcement](https://www.ucc.ie/en/sefs/news/2022/robots-will-help-students-with-disabilities-complete-lab-classes.html)
- **Closest physical-plus-simulation precedent — MIT synthetic biology.** Students edited protocols, simulated them locally and remotely operated an OT-2 with live cameras. TAs loaded reagents, removed samples and ran procedures the robot could not handle. The course report documents this workflow and the staffing behind it. It offers no direct comparison for students with motor disabilities. Stanford researchers also paired live student-controlled experiments on microorganisms with a model. Of 39 learners answering a preference question, 72% gave a reason in favor of the live lab, while others found simulation adequate. The course was not a controlled comparison of learning outcomes by format. [MIT course report, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-022-01601-x.pdf) · [Hossain et al., biology cloud lab](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-017-0150-3)
- **Closest robot-arm teaching precedent — TeleopLab.** Six students used a phone-controlled robot arm in remote tensile-testing labs. The study documents a learning curve for operating the system. The pilot's published description does not establish access for students with upper-limb motor disabilities, and a smartphone interface can itself require precise movement. [TeleopLab study and pilot details](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05547) · [W3C guidance on motor access](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/abilities-barriers/physical/)

**Product hypothesis.** A student may gain control when an accessible interface lets them choose a consequential action, verify what actually happened and decide what to do next, with less need to wait for or negotiate each action. The benefit would be strongest when the course values the student's own observations or decisions about actual material, and an adapted instrument or well-run assistant arrangement leaves a gap the student cares about. It would weaken if an assistant already preserves the same decisions with less effort, a fixture restores direct handling, or simulation meets the student's goals with fewer delays. A robot running a fixed sequence can leave the student with less control than an assistant who follows specific instructions and lets the student learn from their own mistakes. The evidence supports testing this comparison for a particular task. [Butler case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [Rocca adaptation case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408490932_Adaptation_of_laboratory_equipment_for_use_by_students_with_disabilities) · [Physical versus virtual inquiry meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1163024)

## 4. Provisional users and adoption

These are interview profiles based on documented experiences. They describe task needs that can overlap within the same person.

| Provisional student profile | Need to investigate | What could make OpenArms unnecessary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Student who handles some steps and needs help with precision or repetition.** | Wants to stay at the shared bench and retain control over the pace and decisions when sustained pipetting, pouring or gripping becomes difficult. | A suitable pipette, stabilizing fixture, changes to labware or occasional directed help may already cover the task. [Mesecar](https://stemwithdisabilities.squarespace.com/stories/monica-mesecar) · [Paparella](https://ncas.ac.uk/towards-more-accessible-laboratory-practices-with-alyssa-paparella/) |
| **Student who regularly directs someone else for physical execution.** | Wants to initiate, inspect and revise their own procedure, with a control method they can operate reliably. Their preference for working through a person or a device remains an interview question. | A trained assistant can cover more of the protocol, adapt to irregularities and support participation alongside classmates. An instrument-specific adaptation may already deliver direct operation. [Butler case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [Rocca adaptation case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408490932_Adaptation_of_laboratory_equipment_for_use_by_students_with_disabilities) |
| **Student whose attendance or stamina fluctuates.** | Wants to take breaks, catch up after an absence or complete the assessed work at a manageable pace. This profile helps test the limits of the robot's relevance. | A flexible make-up, rest breaks or an accessible asynchronous lab may address the main barrier. A robot with a fixed booking slot may reproduce the scheduling problem. [Ogden, p. 30](https://openlibrary-repo.ecampusontario.ca/jspui/bitstream/123456789/1689/1/Making-Lab-Based-Courses-Inclusive-1669839520.pdf#page=36) · [Remote laboratory needs study](https://doi.org/10.1017/pds.2026.10654) |

- **Instructor or course coordinator — owns the learning and grading decision.** Ask which parts of a practical require the student to design, observe, troubleshoot or interpret, and which require direct technique or safety practice. ACS guidelines for certified chemistry degrees call for at least 350 in-person lab hours beyond introductory experiences, with foundational and in-depth virtual or at-home work serving as supplements. Whether an in-room, robot-assisted step meets a given course outcome requires a course-level determination. ASM's undergraduate microbiology recommendations likewise include both specimen preparation and analysis. [ACS laboratory curriculum and skills](https://www.acs.org/education/policies/acs-approval-program/guidelines/laboratory.html) · [ASM curriculum guidelines](https://asm.org/guideline/asm-curriculum-guidelines-for-undergraduate-microb)
- **Disability-services professional — coordinates an individualized accommodation with the student and course.** The office needs timely notice, a clear account of the task barrier and a way to check whether the accommodation works in practice. NJIT publicly describes case-by-case accommodation and lists adaptive equipment. UConn documents an assistant model. Which option a campus can offer promptly and who pays can differ. [NJIT Office of Accessibility Resources and Services](https://www.njit.edu/accessibility/accommodations-and-support-services) · [UConn assistant policy](https://disabilities.center.uconn.edu/accommodations/accommodation-types/lab-and-discussion-assistants/) · [US Department of Education guidance on individualized academic adjustments](https://www.ed.gov/higher-education/students-disabilities-preparing-postsecondary-education)
- **TA, lab manager and environmental health and safety staff — own day-to-day operation and safety review.** A lab would need to establish who prepares and resets the station, supervises the run, handles spills or equipment failures, and keeps the student on track if a run is interrupted. Added setup, cleaning, calibration, staff training and queue time could offset time saved on manipulation. The MIT precedent explicitly used TA loading and retrieval and campus safety review. Accessible controls also need to cover stopping and recovery when speech, network or feedback fails. [MIT course report](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-022-01601-x.pdf) · [ACS academic laboratory safety guidance](https://www.acs.org/content/dam/pldp/center/lab-safety/publications/acs-safety-guidelines-academic.pdf) · [University of Washington mobility and lab safety guidance](https://doit.uw.edu/knowledge-base/what-lab-safety-issues-need-to-be-considered-when-working-with-a-student-with-a-mobility-impairment)
- **Department, accessibility office or teaching-innovation fund — possible budget holders.** Butler used disability-services funds for an assistant. Cork used a digital-inclusion initiative for its biochemistry robot and separately reported €16,277 in funding routed through disability support for a wheelchair-accessible chemistry bench, sink and extraction system. These are examples of distinct spending decisions. A buyer would need to compare full cost per usable session, including people, supplies, upkeep and interruptions, with its current accommodation. Digital accessibility review may also be part of procurement, as Rutgers documents. The funding owner and willingness to pay for OpenArms are unverified. [Butler case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [Cork robot project](https://www.ucc.ie/en/sefs/news/2022/robots-will-help-students-with-disabilities-complete-lab-classes.html) · [Cork facilities case](https://www.ucc.ie/en/chemistry/about/edi/casestudies/) · [Rutgers accessibility purchasing process](https://it.rutgers.edu/digital-accessibility/purchaseform/)

**Voice is a preference and an access question.** Mesecar found speech-to-text useful for some work. In the small AccessScope evaluation, six of seven participants used a keyboard and one used speech recognition, with multiple options available. A 27-participant study of people with speech disorders found recognition performance varied with speech mode and model. It did not measure robot command accuracy. Relevant interview and observation conditions include background noise, speaking in front of peers, fatigue, the person's usual assistive technology, and whether they can verify, correct or stop an action. A keyboard-compatible path can support some switch and alternative inputs. Its usability depends on the student's actual setup. [Mesecar](https://stemwithdisabilities.squarespace.com/stories/monica-mesecar) · [AccessScope](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~bsd/papers/duerstock_2006.html) · [Speech recognition study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12379580/) · [W3C input guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/tools-techniques/input/)

## 5. Customer discovery

Start with recent lab experiences and introduce the product concept after participants describe the current process. Include students who are satisfied with their accommodations, students with remaining barriers, and where feasible students who avoided or withdrew from a lab. Interview students separately from staff who grade them. Use participants' preferred communication format and ask about functional tasks without requiring a diagnosis. A syllabus, lab handout or anonymized accommodation workflow can anchor the discussion in a real case.

| Who | Interview question | Assumption it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | **1. Walk me through your most recent lab, from getting to the bench through cleanup. Which steps did you do, direct, watch or skip? Who decided what happened next?** | The barrier is at a specific action and affects control, participation or completion. |
| Student | **2. Tell me about the last time you tried a different tool, worked with a partner or used an assistant or alternative assignment. What worked well? Where did you still wait, change the plan or miss practice you wanted?** | The best existing accommodation leaves a meaningful gap. |
| Student | **3. In that lab, when did it matter to make your own measurement, repeat a step or respond to an unexpected result? What could you learn from a supplied dataset or a simulation?** | Live student-directed experimentation adds something the student values for that assignment. |
| Student | **4. Which controls did you use successfully in class or on a device you rely on? When did they become difficult? How did you check what the system understood and correct or cancel an action?** | A usable input, feedback and recovery path exists under real conditions. |
| Instructor or coordinator | **5. For the last practical you adapted, what did each student have to demonstrate for credit? Which steps could be carried out under student direction, and what evidence did you accept for grading?** | The proposed workflow can meet a defined learning outcome and yield assessable evidence. |
| Disability services | **6. Walk me through a recent lab accommodation request without identifying the student. Which options were considered, what was delivered by the first lab, and how did you learn whether it worked?** | There is a recurring gap in timeliness or fit and a route to approve a different option. |
| TA, lab manager or safety staff | **7. Think of the last new device or procedure you introduced to a teaching lab. Who set it up, supervised it and handled problems? What happened when a station failed during a section?** | The campus can support preparation, supervision, recovery and maintenance within a normal section. |
| Department or budget owner | **8. What did the department fund the last time it faced a comparable access or equipment need? Who paid for setup and ongoing support, what alternatives were considered, and what evidence justified spending or declining?** | A real funding path exists, with decision criteria a prototype could eventually meet. |

**After the baseline discussion**, present the same course task under the participant's existing accommodation, a directed human assistant, an accessible simulation and a student-controlled physical device, where each is feasible. Keep the learning objective and the student's choices constant. Ask what each option would let them do, what would still require another person, which they would choose for that assignment, and what would change their choice. When observation is possible, record who initiated and verified each step, help requests, time waiting, recovery from an error, access to group discussion, and staff minutes for setup and reset. Include a course-specific learning check so that completing a movement and demonstrating the intended skill are measured separately.

**What remains unknown.** The literature does not establish which barriers recur at a likely first campus, how many remain after a good accommodation, which students would choose the robot after trying alternatives, or whether benefits persist beyond a demonstration. Comparative evidence would weaken the product case if students can already make the same decisions with less effort, if the live result changes little about what they learn, or if staffing and downtime make the option less dependable. A named course owner, a clear assessment criterion and an identified source of ongoing support would provide stronger adoption evidence than general enthusiasm.

## What this changes for OpenArms

1. **Validate a specific unresolved task against the student's best available accommodation.** Start with students who can name a recent step where they lost control, practice or access after accommodations were in place. A trained assistant and adapted equipment are credible benchmarks. If either already gives the student the control they want with lower effort, that task offers little reason to add a robot. [Butler case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [Rocca adaptation case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408490932_Adaptation_of_laboratory_equipment_for_use_by_students_with_disabilities)
2. **Make the prototype's accessibility claim about a student completing the full interaction.** The evidence to seek is whether a student can use a preferred input, including a usable nonvoice option, to make a meaningful choice, verify the observed outcome and correct or stop an action. Ask the instructor whether that interaction demonstrates the chosen learning outcome. If the evidence only shows the arm moving, describe it as a hardware demonstration. [AccessScope evaluation](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~bsd/papers/duerstock_2006.html) · [W3C input guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/tools-techniques/input/) · [ACS laboratory skills](https://www.acs.org/education/policies/acs-approval-program/guidelines/laboratory.html)
3. **Test the case for one supervised teaching-lab use before making a broader adoption claim.** For a real course, ask the instructor, accessibility office and lab staff what benefit would justify a trial and who would own preparation, supervision and recovery. Use actual workflow and staffing evidence to judge whether real equipment earns its place alongside simulation. Cork and MIT show that curriculum adaptation and staff support are part of the cost of delivery. [Cork biochemistry project](https://www.ucc.ie/en/sefs/news/2022/robots-will-help-students-with-disabilities-complete-lab-classes.html) · [MIT course report](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-022-01601-x.pdf)
