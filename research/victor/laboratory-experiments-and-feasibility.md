# Which laboratory experiments can OpenArms support with the SO-101?

**HackPrinceton research report · November 14, 2026**

**Evidence:** Published procedures, hardware documentation, and desk research. Bench trials pending.

**Test controlled buoyancy first.** A student can choose matched samples, predict how mass and volume affect floating, direct the arm to release a sample into stationary water, and verify a real outcome. The same grasp, transport, and release sequence could start an effervescent-tablet experiment that measures how water temperature affects fizz duration. Qualitative pH remains a strong chemistry option if a mounted test strip can be dipped and read reliably, or if a separately validated dispenser delivers a prepared portion. The arm’s first useful capabilities are moving a known object between fixed stations and releasing it into a fixture. Add a repeatable dip or fixture-triggered dose when a lesson needs it. [UW–Madison buoyancy lab](https://education.mrsec.wisc.edu/forces-of-fluids-density-and-buoyancy/) · [ACS Fizz Race](https://www.acs.org/education/celebrating-chemistry-editions/2021-ncw/fizz-race.html) · [University of Maine pH activity](https://extension.umaine.edu/4h/stem-toolkits/your-future-in-aquaculture-testing-the-waters/activity-5-ph/)

The shared OpenArms analysis proposed a three-vial pH investigation with prefilled portions, buoyancy as a fallback, and simulation for both motion previews and experiments the arm cannot perform. It also described the mandarin transfer as a hardware milestone. This report treats each proposal as a hypothesis. It draws on published teaching procedures, manufacturer and open-hardware documentation, and accessibility research. The apparatus adaptations, prototype estimates, and acceptance thresholds below are proposals. No OpenArms arm, fixture, reagent, or student workflow was tested for this report. ## 1. Experiment opportunity landscape

### 1. Controlled buoyancy and average density · Physics

**Question and objective.** At the same external volume, can a difference in mass determine whether an object floats? Relate average density to floating and sinking, then use the relationship to predict the behavior of a third sample. **Student decisions.** Choose a matched pair, decide what to hold constant, predict both outcomes from mass and volume, and choose a repeat or a third sample to check the explanation. **Bench and result.** Use lightweight, rigid, sealed samples with a common grip shape, a stationary clear plastic tank, a balance, and external volumes measured in advance or from known dimensions. Release each just above the water surface, clear the arm, and observe its stable position. Include two same-volume samples with masses safely on opposite sides of the floating threshold. For illustration, a sealed 40 mL sample could have a total mass of 30 g or 50 g. Include the grip tab in both mass and external volume. An equal-mass, different-volume pair adds a second test if both fit the fixture. The standard university practical also measures displaced water and apparent weight. Float/sink observations test the density prediction. A quantitative verification of Archimedes’ principle requires those extra measurements or equivalent instrumentation. [UW–Madison forces of fluids practical](https://education.mrsec.wisc.edu/forces-of-fluids-density-and-buoyancy/) · [OpenStax, Archimedes’ principle](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-4-archimedes-principle-and-buoyancy)

### 2. Temperature and effervescence · Chemistry

**Question and objective.** How does starting water temperature affect the time an effervescent tablet visibly produces bubbles? Practice changing one variable, timing an operational endpoint, and interpreting a proxy for a process rate. **Student decisions.** Choose the temperature comparison and trial order, make a prediction, select a repeat, and decide whether the difference exceeds run-to-run variation. **Bench and result.** A fixed holder seats clear plastic cups with the same water volume at two measured, venue-approved temperatures, starting with cool and room temperature. Release identical whole tablets from a dry carrier or shallow chute, record actual water contact, and compare time until sustained bubbling stops. Keep formulation, tablet mass, geometry, water volume, and endpoint rule constant. The result is a duration under defined conditions. Dissolution, reaction, dissolved CO₂, and bubble release all affect it. The activity can teach rate comparisons without claiming that a stopwatch measures intrinsic reaction kinetics or total CO₂. [ACS Fizz Race procedure](https://www.acs.org/education/celebrating-chemistry-editions/2021-ncw/fizz-race.html) · [Science Buddies gas-displacement measurement extension](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/project-ideas/Chem_p026/chemistry/how-fast-does-an-alka-seltzer-tablet-make-gas)

### 3. Qualitative pH and indicator response · Chemistry

**Question and objective.** Which prepared aqueous unknowns are acidic, near neutral, or basic, and which reference results justify that classification? Connect an indicator response to acid–base behavior and use controls to check the method. **Student decisions.** Choose a comparison standard and unknown, predict a category, compare the reading with the controls, and decide whether to repeat an ambiguous result. **Bench and result.** Put clear, venue-approved samples in fixed cups. For the simplest arm trial, use a fresh disposable pH strip on a rigid, lightweight tab for each sample. Dip to the maker’s specified depth and duration, lift to a marked reading station, and read within the specified window. Provide a measured pH band or a text/spoken reading of the observed strip or calibrated meter, so the student can interpret it. If the learning objective includes seeing an indicator change in solution, use a fixed receiver and a measured, fixture-assisted dose with fresh vessels. The ACS cabbage activity uses household solutions and a dropper. Its universal-indicator lesson also shows that selected dry reagents affect the indicator after dissolving. A dry-reagent adaptation would investigate how a solid changes the pH of water, a different question from classifying an existing aqueous unknown. [ACS red cabbage indicator procedure](https://www.acs.org/education/activities/red-cabbage-indicator.html) · [ACS pH and color change](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/lessonplans/chapter6/lesson8.html) · [University of Maine pH activity](https://extension.umaine.edu/4h/stem-toolkits/your-future-in-aquaculture-testing-the-waters/activity-5-ph/)

### 4. Balancing torques with a lever · Physics

**Question and objective.** Can a lighter mass balance a heavier one by sitting farther from the pivot? Use signed torques and equilibrium to predict placement or infer an unknown mass. **Student decisions.** Choose a mass and a marked distance, predict direction of tilt or balance, then adjust the arrangement and explain the result. **Bench and result.** Use a light, initially balanced beam, captive pivot and travel stops, marked pockets, and small captive or tabbed weights. Place known masses into broad pockets and clear the arm before reading left tilt, right tilt, or balance. A rigid loading fixture or temporary beam lock can hold the receiving pocket in place during loading. The arm must release any lock before the reading. Measure the pivot-to-load-center distance. Friction and a center-of-mass offset can mask small differences in torque. [Clemson torque and equilibrium lab](https://science.clemson.edu/physics/labs/labs/207/torque/index.html) · [OpenStax, static equilibrium](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/12-1-conditions-for-static-equilibrium)

### 5. Dye dilution and concentration · Chemistry

**Question and objective.** How much water makes a known stock one-half or one-quarter as concentrated? Use conservation of solute to plan a dilution, then distinguish concentration from appearance. **Student decisions.** Choose a target, calculate a dose from known stock and starting volume, choose a blank and reference, and revise the plan using the measurement. **Bench and result.** Use stationary receivers, labeled dye stock, premeasured water portions, controlled mixing, and a balance or calibrated volume measurements. For the first version, hold dye quantity constant and add water to duplicate fresh receivers. Measure with identical optical path lengths, for example with fixed cuvettes, if absorbance is part of the lesson. A reliable mass-based estimate or validated colorimeter can give an independently checkable result. Apparent darkness through vessels of different depth is ambiguous. Serial dilution adds aliquot transfers, mixing between stages, and accumulating errors. [ChemCollective dilution tutorial](https://chemcollective.org/activities/tutorials/stoich/dilution) · [AACT Beer’s law lab](https://teachchemistry.org/classroom-resources/beer-s-law-discovered) · [OpenStax, molarity and dilution](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/3-3-molarity)

### 6. Osmosis across a membrane · Biology

**Question and objective.** How does a sucrose concentration difference across dialysis tubing affect the direction and size of mass change over time? Explain net water movement across a selectively permeable barrier and compare against an equal-concentration control. **Student decisions.** Choose the internal/external concentrations from prepared options, predict direction, select time points, and judge whether the results exceed weighing and blotting error. **Bench and result.** Staff prepare, seal, leak-check, and initially weigh the bags. A rigid tab can let the arm lower a bag into a bath and retrieve it without squeezing it. Standardized blotting and tared weighing are assisted steps unless the team validates them. The observation is mass change from baseline, optionally normalized by initial mass. Wet surfaces, leaks, and inconsistent immersion can mimic the signal. The reference samples bags at 10-minute intervals for an hour. One live placement plus clearly labeled prior measurements can support a short demo without compressing the biological timescale. [CUNY City Tech osmosis practical](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/bio-oer/osmosis-and-diffusion/observing-osmosis/) · [OpenStax Biology, passive transport](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-2-passive-transport)

### 7. Yeast fermentation and sugar choice · Biology

**Question and objective.** How does available sugar affect CO₂ evolution in a prepared baker’s-yeast assay? Use a no-added-sugar control and a time series to interpret metabolic activity under defined conditions. **Student decisions.** Choose a substrate and control, hold yeast quantity, temperature, and the stated sugar concentration basis constant, and interpret lag and variation between replicates. **Bench and result.** Use staff-prepared yeast suspensions and labeled sugar portions with a fixed, supervised gas-measurement station. The arm might add a premeasured sugar portion or place a prepared sample into the station. Staff assemble and reset the gas collection apparatus. The Rockefeller protocol reads CO₂ displacement over 30 minutes. Use its defined collection method or a validated alternative if comparing rates. A balloon or foam height can offer a qualitative cue with substantial measurement limitations. CO₂ under unspecified aeration is insufficient evidence to distinguish fermentation from respiration. [Rockefeller University RockEDU fermentation protocol](https://rockedu.rockefeller.edu/stem-activities/fermentation-of-food/testing-substrate-specificity-yeast-fermentation/) · [Penn State fermentation balloon module](https://aese.psu.edu/teachag/curriculum/modules/fermentation/experiment-fermentation-balloons/introductory)

### 8. Acid–base titration of an unknown · Chemistry

**Question and objective.** What is the concentration of a known monoprotic acid of unknown molarity? Use a standardized base, recorded volumes, and stoichiometry to infer concentration, and distinguish a measured endpoint from the equivalence point. **Student decisions.** Choose increment sizes, reduce them near the expected equivalence region, choose how to infer the endpoint, and repeat or explain discrepant results. **Bench and result.** A physical lesson needs a burette or calibrated dispenser, accurate initial aliquot, mixing, and a validated indicator or pH probe. A gripper can plausibly move a carrier into a fixed station after testing. The quantitative part requires dedicated lab equipment and appropriate reagent handling. An acid–base color change alone provides insufficient evidence of a quantitative titration. An accessible physical precedent uses a drop counter and spoken pH/volume data with adapted instruments. [UBC/ChemCollective titration practical](https://chemcollective.org/chem/ubc/exp06/) · [OpenStax titration curves](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-7-acid-base-titrations) · [Perkins School for the Blind nonvisual titration](https://www.perkins.org/resource/acid-base-titration-using-nonvisual-tools/)

## 2. Experiment comparison matrix

Difficulty and time are engineering estimates for a first supervised prototype, assuming the follower is mounted, calibrated, and controllable and the materials are on hand. **Short** is roughly 1–3 hours of apparatus setup and first trials, **medium** 3–6 hours, and **long** beyond that or dependent on specialist equipment. Qualification and assay elapsed time are separate. Access value reflects how much difficult handling the proposed arm action takes over and how independently the student can read the outcome. Evidence quality concerns the lesson and scientific basis. OpenArms execution evidence is pending for all eight.

| Experiment | Learning objective and consequential choice | Equipment and arm action | Physical difficulty and time | Accessibility value and demo clarity | Simulation potential | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Buoyancy** | Test density by choosing matched samples and a follow-up | Tabbed solids, fixed water tank, mass and volume data. Move and release. | Low · short | High if the learner can select and verify the outcome. Stable float/sink result is quick to read. | High. Independent mass, volume, fluid density and force. | University lab and textbook. Simplified adaptation tests a narrower claim. |
| **Effervescence** | Isolate temperature and evaluate timed repeat trials | Dry tablets, cup holder, thermometer and timer. Move and release or trigger chute. | Low–medium · short | High if elapsed time and endpoint evidence are accessible. Strong paired result if distributions separate. | Moderate. Measured curves over tested conditions. | Direct ACS procedure and separate gas-measurement exercise. Proposed cooler range needs testing. |
| **Qualitative pH** | Use references to classify an unknown and decide when to repeat | Fixed sample cups, strip tab and reading station. Move, dip and lift. Optional measured dose. | Medium · medium for dip. Higher for a carried pour. | High for wet test handling. Observation access depends on a validated color-to-text or measured reading. | High. Bench-matched categories and controlled acid–base equilibrium. | ACS and university procedures. Mount and readout untested. |
| **Torque** | Predict or infer balance by choosing mass and position | Beam, captive weights, scale marks and travel stops. Move and seat. | Low–medium · short to medium | Medium–high. Fully dry and legible if tilt/balance is reported accessibly. | High. Signed torque, variable lever arms and unknown masses. | Direct university lab and textbook. Beam fixture needs validation. |
| **Dye dilution** | Calculate and verify a target concentration | Fixed receiver, portions, mixing and independent measurement. Trigger or tip. | Medium–high · medium to long | High for a genuine dose choice. Readout needs mass/volume or calibrated optics. | High. Mass balance, serial dilution and uncertainty. | Established instruction and lab. Physical dose and optical readings are open. |
| **Osmosis** | Predict direction and compare mass change with a control | Bags, baths, blotting and balance. Lower, retrieve and seat. | Medium–high · medium plus soak | Medium with staff blotting and weighing. Weak real-time result in a brief demo. | High with measured time series. | Direct university procedure. Timing and mass-handling demands are explicit. |
| **Fermentation** | Compare substrate effects against a no-added-sugar control | Yeast, sugar portions, controlled temperature and gas collection. Add or seat. | Medium–high · medium to long plus incubation | Medium with staff collection setup. Result develops over tens of minutes. | High within measured conditions. | Direct university outreach protocol. Assay variability and interpretation matter. |
| **Titration** | Infer concentration and explain endpoint choice | Standardized titrant, calibrated delivery, mixing, pH or endpoint readout. Seat sample only unless using an independently validated dispenser. | High · long | High for accurate, student-directed instrument use. Weak demo if reduced to a color change. | High. Validatable curves and adaptive dosing. | University tutorial, textbook and accessible instrument demonstration. |

Buoyancy, effervescence, and pH combine a consequential student choice, observable evidence, and a manageable manipulation. Torque is close and moves ahead of pH when the bench must stay dry or the pH readout is unreliable. Dye dilution and titration have strong quantitative teaching value, with measurement requirements that raise the physical cost. Osmosis and fermentation are meaningful for biology, with time and staff preparation that constrain a short live session.

| Topic | What the documentation supports | What OpenArms must measure or confirm |
|---|---|---|
| **Hardware identity** | The Hiwonder listing specifies a PLA follower, 111 × 173 × 532 mm overall, with six HX-30HM servos and a 12 V, 5 A supply for that kit. The original designer describes lower- and higher-voltage follower variants. | **Reach and orientation** | A separate Robonine successor comparison lists 441 mm horizontal tip radius for its SO-ARM101 comparison. The nominal geometry is useful for scale. The usable reach for a downward grip, vertical dip, or tilted vessel includes orientation and clearance. [Robonine comparison and conditions](https://github.com/roboninecom/SO-ARM-102/blob/main/docs/specifications.md) · [Original SO-101 geometry](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/blob/main/Simulation/SO101/README.md) | Map reachable source, release, reading and withdrawal positions in the intended orientation. | **Payload and deflection** | Robonine lists 150 g rated payload for the SO-ARM101 comparison and reports 7.5 mm frame deflection under a 300 g test load at full extension, with printed servo dummies in joints 2–4. This isolated frame measurement and the comparison rating do not establish the Hiwonder kit’s working payload. Servo stall torque is a motor rating. [Robonine test conditions](https://github.com/roboninecom/SO-ARM-102/blob/main/docs/specifications.md) · [Hiwonder servo data](https://www.hiwonder.com/products/hx-30hm) | Weigh each loaded assembly, including tab or carrier. Check hold, slip, sag, clearance, and temperature or faults if available, at actual orientations, speeds and trial cadence. Test the planned load first at the nearest station before increasing reach. The receiver stays on the bench, so only the sample and carrier contribute to the lifted load. |
| **Grip, precision and repeatability** | The reference follower has five positioning joints and a gripper with one moving jaw. The vendor and LeRobot describe servo position feedback, joint calibration and teleoperation. The gripper command scale expresses opening. [Hiwonder manual](https://wiki.hiwonder.com/projects/LeRobot/en/latest/docs/1_SO_ARM101_Open_Source_6_Axis_Robotic_Arm_User_Manual.html) · [TheRobotStudio gripper mapping](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/blob/main/Simulation/SO101/README.md) · [Robonine comparison](https://github.com/roboninecom/SO-ARM-102/blob/main/docs/specifications.md) | Measure jaw opening and part slip separately from tip position. Use a target and camera or jig to measure both offset from the desired position and scatter around the repeated position, from multiple approaches and across resets. Fit clearance to the measured loaded path. |
| **Liquid handling** | Wrist motion makes tilting geometrically possible. The cited kit documentation describes a gripper and position control. Dose, spill containment, repeatable mixing, and liquid-level or volume sensing remain unverified. Research demonstrations of accurate robotic pouring use feedback to address flow and tilt. Purpose-built liquid handlers publish accuracy and precision by pipette and volume. [Hiwonder manual](https://wiki.hiwonder.com/projects/LeRobot/en/latest/docs/1_SO_ARM101_Open_Source_6_Axis_Robotic_Arm_User_Manual.html) · [Robotic pouring study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02610) · [Opentrons pipette specifications](https://docs.opentrons.com/ot-2/pipettes/) | Test a supported, fixture-triggered dose with water first. If testing a carried cup, include the wet grasp, retention at tilt, delivered amount, residue, spillage, return, and the next trial. An open cup can lose liquid despite a successful position command. |

A 2026 SO-101 preprint benchmark shows that fixtures, camera placement, and operator resets matter even on standardized tabletop tasks. Its seven policies were trained or fine-tuned with 50 demonstrations per task and performed very differently across tasks. It supplies evidence that SO-101 cells can perform several kinds of manipulation under the authors’ conditions. Its success rates do not estimate performance for this arm, these fixtures, or scripted motions. [ArmnetBench v0.1 methods and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1)

**Practical bench adaptation.** Clamp the arm base and fix the source and destination relative to it. Use indexed nests and broad receivers, lightweight rigid plastic vessels and carriers with flat grip tabs, and enough cup headspace for low drops. Keep the tank or receiving vessel stationary, with secondary containment and electronics outside the potential spill path. Staff load, refill, clean, and recover objects between runs unless those steps have their own validation. Use only materials and temperatures allowed at the venue. For fizz, use open receiving cups. For pH, use approved dilute preparations and a fresh wetted component per sample. Run dry checks and then water-only mechanical trials before considering reagents. Confirm how the supervised stop behaves, including any sag or release after stopping, before students direct a physical trial. [Hiwonder setup and calibration guidance](https://wiki.hiwonder.com/projects/LeRobot/en/latest/docs/1_SO_ARM101_Open_Source_6_Axis_Robotic_Arm_User_Manual.html) · [ACS risk assessment guidance](https://institute.acs.org/acs-center/lab-safety/safety-basics-and-ramp/what-is-ramp.html)

## 4. Reusable laboratory actions

| Capability | What a reusable version requires | Experiments it enables |
|---|---|---|
| **Pick up and move a prepared item** | Known source and destination, registered source nest, compatible grip tab, measured mass and center of mass within the tested range, adequate clearance. Verify that the selected item arrived at the selected station. | Base action for all eight. Directly useful for the buoyancy sample, dry tablet, strip carrier, torque weight and sample carriers. |
| **Seat or release into a fixed receiver** | Broad target, low release height, sufficient rim clearance, and evidence that the item cleared the gripper. A receiving pocket, tray or chute can guide the item. | Starts buoyancy and effervescence. Seats weights for torque and prepared carriers for instrument readings. |
| **Lower, dwell and lift** | Calibrated vertical clearance, rigid tab, known immersion depth and dwell time, and a clean or single-use contact surface. Keep the gripper dry. | Dips pH strips, starts or ends an osmosis exposure, and could support quantitative buoyancy with a separate force or displacement measurement. |
| **Deliver one prepared portion using a fixed device** | Stationary receiver and a constrained tip or dispenser action. Verify the delivered amount and the return state. Report counts only for validated portions, with a fresh or cleaned contact path as needed. | Supports indicator mixing, dye dilution and yeast substrate addition. Precision titration would still require an independently calibrated dispensing and measurement instrument. |
| **Wait and observe** | A timer tied to the actual event and a camera, sensor, or clearly attributed human reading. Keep the observation available for review and comparison. | Every activity. Waiting and observing are experiment capabilities. The arm can move clear so the student can read the result. |

A fixed source nest, common gripping surface, and shared approach/release region could let buoyancy and effervescence use the same handling sequence. A disposable tablet carrier may add a step if tablets chip or stick. A tank or cup exchange also changes rim height, splash exposure and withdrawal clearance. Reuse the motion where the fixture and payload remain inside the measured envelope, then rerun the acceptance check for the new receiver. The pH dip adds an approach, controlled dwell, and withdrawal at the same class of indexed station. Neither reuse claim depends on a trained policy or on finding objects scattered across the bench. ### Where the access benefit comes from

A lab can delegate lifting, reaching or fine manipulation while preserving the learner’s selection of variables, timing, measurement, and interpretation. In a purposively sampled survey, Jeannis and colleagues found that students with physical disabilities reported being assigned passive lab roles, and many reported that peers helped them complete tasks. In one undergraduate organic lab case, a student directed an assistant through difficult equipment operations. A University of the Republic case used adapted equipment to support independent completion of required practicals at ordinary accuracy tolerances. University College Cork’s OT-2 project adapted procedures for four biochemistry classes and kept calculations and sample choices with the student. These sources support the barrier and demonstrate alternatives. The access advantage of an SO-101 for this audience remains a testable hypothesis. The Uruguay study has a published correction whose full text could not be verified for this report. [Jeannis et al. survey](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30729844/) · [Esteb et al. teaching-lab case](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291152783_Accommodations_in_the_Organic_Teaching_Laboratory_for_Students_with_Mobility_Issues) · [González Muzio et al. equipment adaptations](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6SU90035F) · [Full-text copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408490932_Adaptation_of_laboratory_equipment_for_use_by_students_with_disabilities) · [Correction record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1039/d6su90055k) · [University College Cork project](https://www.ucc.ie/en/sefs/news/2022/robots-will-help-students-with-disabilities-complete-lab-classes.html)

For the prototype, the learner should be able to select a control and experimental condition, confirm the action, review a real observation, request a repeat, and stop or request a stop through an input they can use. Offer equivalent non-voice input where feasible, plus readable status and measured results in text or speech. For color-based experiments, report the observed color or pH band and let the learner classify it. For fizz and buoyancy, report time or stable sample position with a way to review the source observation. A staff reading can serve in a supervised prototype if it is identified as such. Test the same task with an adapted fixture, a directed assistant, or accessible simulation where practical. Compare who makes and verifies decisions, how much help is needed, and what evidence the learner can access. [Penn State science-lab accommodations](https://equity.psu.edu/offices/student-disability-resources/accommodations/implementation-guides/science-labs) · [W3C use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) · [W3C keyboard access](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html) · [Perkins nonvisual measurement example](https://www.perkins.org/resource/acid-base-titration-using-nonvisual-tools/)

## 5. Physical and simulated experiments

A **motion preview** shows the intended sample, station and motion. TheRobotStudio supplies SO-101 URDF and MuJoCo assets with different joint-zero conventions and a documented mismatch with LeRobot’s gripper mapping. The model also omits base collision meshes. Match the preview to the actual setup and verify real clearance independently. An **experiment model** predicts a physical outcome from stated variables or uses identified reference measurements. A video replay or a recorded dataset is useful evidence when labeled with its source, conditions and date. Animation alone provides no basis for a new scientific prediction. Label each result as live measurement, recorded measurement, or model prediction, and expose its assumptions or uncertainty. [TheRobotStudio SO-101 simulation notes](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/blob/main/Simulation/SO101/README.md) · [Stanford biology cloud-lab study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-017-0150-3)

| Experiment | Physical contribution and constraint | Virtual extension and evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Buoyancy** | Run matched samples in stationary water. Drops, trapped air, leakage, wall contact, or surface tension on small samples can obscure the density relationship. | Independently vary mass, external volume, and fluid density. Use object density `m/V` and `F_b = ρ_fluid g V_displaced`. For an ideal freely floating body, submerged fraction is `ρ_object/ρ_fluid`. Check against measured samples and a force-balance reference. Specify the neutral limit and disregard transient splash or drag unless modeled. [OpenStax buoyancy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-4-archimedes-principle-and-buoyancy) · [PhET Buoyancy](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/buoyancy) |
| **Effervescence** | Compare timed runs at two approved temperatures with equal tablets and water volumes. Very short runs, a vague endpoint or overlapping durations can make the comparison inconclusive. | Start with replicated, measured duration or CO₂-volume traces for the exact formulation, geometry, volume and temperatures. Label measured trials and any interpolation. A predictive time course outside the measured range would need a calibrated dissolution/reaction and gas-release model. An ideal rate law alone does not predict visible fizz. [ACS Fizz Race](https://www.acs.org/education/celebrating-chemistry-editions/2021-ncw/fizz-race.html) · [Gas-displacement procedure](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/project-ideas/Chem_p026/chemistry/how-fast-does-an-alka-seltzer-tablet-make-gas) |
| **Qualitative pH** | Dip fresh strips in a few known controls and clear unknowns, or perform one validated indicator mix. Resolve ambiguous bands against a reference measurement. | Use measured pH and indicator appearance for those preparations. For additional defined acids, bases and dilution, solve acid–base equilibria using species, concentrations, volumes, `Ka`/`Kb` or `pKa`, and water autoionization. An ideal dilute model can approximate activity by concentration. Add measured color bands for the actual indicator under stated conditions. Household mixtures or buffers need composition or reference measurements. pH values cannot be combined by arithmetic averaging. [OpenStax, pH and pOH](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-2-ph-and-poh) · [OpenStax acid–base curves](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-7-acid-base-titrations) · [PhET pH Scale](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/ph-scale) |
| **Torque** | Use large, reproducible torque differences and calibrated pocket positions. Check friction, beam tare and whether weights stay seated. | Let students vary mass, distance and fulcrum position, or infer a hidden mass. Compute signed moments `m g r_perp` and check `Στ = 0` at equilibrium. Include beam mass if its center of mass is offset. A static model supports balance and tilt predictions. Predicting swing or settling time requires dynamics and friction. [OpenStax static equilibrium](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/12-1-conditions-for-static-equilibrium) · [PhET Balancing Act](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/balancing-act) |
| **Dye dilution** | Verify delivery gravimetrically or volumetrically and mix each receiver. Measure optical results using a blank and fixed geometry if required. | Support arbitrary target ratios and serial transfers with solute mass balance. Use `C₁V₁ = C₂V₂` for a single stock aliquot diluted to a known final volume and conserve solute at each stage. Model absorbance with `A = εℓc` within a validated linear range at fixed wavelength, or use a measured calibration curve. Use measured dispensing error if simulating uncertainty. [OpenStax molarity](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/3-3-molarity) · [AACT Beer–Lambert procedure](https://teachchemistry.org/classroom-resources/beer-s-law-discovered) · [PhET Concentration](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/concentration) |
| **Osmosis** | Start a real immersion and collect supervised mass measurements when time allows. Identify any prior dataset used in a short demo. | Explore gradient, area and elapsed time. For a quantitative curve, use measured mass-versus-time data for the actual membrane and preparations or fit water flux to permeability, membrane area and osmotic driving force. Account for changing concentrations, pressure or solute leakage where material. Validate against held-out conditions before offering new quantitative predictions. [CUNY City Tech practical](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/bio-oer/osmosis-and-diffusion/observing-osmosis/) · [OpenStax passive transport](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-2-passive-transport) |
| **Fermentation** | Start a supervised live assay when the window and gas collection permit. Use prior identified runs to show longer changes. | Compare measured CO₂ traces by substrate and control for a fixed yeast preparation and conditions. To vary temperature or sugar concentration beyond those data, collect validation data or fit a model with substrate-specific uptake, lag and dependence on assay conditions. Fermentation stoichiometry bounds ideal yield. It does not supply elapsed-time predictions or account for gas lost from the apparatus. [Rockefeller RockEDU protocol](https://rockedu.rockefeller.edu/stem-activities/fermentation-of-food/testing-substrate-specificity-yeast-fermentation/) |
| **Titration** | The SO-101 could present a prepared sample to a station. Accurate aliquots, variable increments, reagent containment, mixing, and endpoint sensing require adapted instruments and supervision. | Let students change increment size, acid strength, concentration, and endpoint method. Use neutralization stoichiometry, mass/charge balance and acid–base equilibria over the whole curve. Check against published strong- and weak-acid examples, including non-neutral equivalence for a weak acid and strong base. Model indicator or sensor error only with documented characteristics. [OpenStax titration curves and tabulated values](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-7-acid-base-titrations) · [UBC/ChemCollective virtual titration](https://chemcollective.org/chem/ubc/exp06/) |

A 35-study meta-analysis found no overall advantage of physical or virtual investigation for conceptual knowledge, with different results under some conditions. It did not compare OpenArms, disability-specific access, or mastery of physical technique. ACS guidance for approved undergraduate chemistry programs treats virtual labs as supplements for foundational and in-depth experiences and encourages adapting physical activities where possible. OpenArms can use live material to teach empirical observation and troubleshooting, and virtual experiments to teach variable control, measurement planning and model-based reasoning. If a course assesses manual instrument technique, the instructor must decide what each mode can demonstrate. [Muilwijk and Lazonder meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1163024) · [ACS undergraduate laboratory guidance](https://www.acs.org/education/policies/acs-approval-program/guidelines/laboratory.html)

## 6. Top three candidates and what would change the ranking

1. **Controlled buoyancy is the strongest first bench candidate.** A controlled pair tests a scientific explanation and a follow-up tests whether it generalizes. It exercises a broad, reusable release into a fixed vessel, leaves liquids stationary, and has a simple, auditable virtual model. Its access value depends on communicating sample mass, volume, and actual float/sink position. It drops from first place if samples cannot be released without wedging, splash or wall contact, the readout is unreliable, or available samples reduce the experience to an obvious prearranged reveal. A dry torque fixture is the closest substitute if any open water is impractical. [UW–Madison buoyancy practical](https://education.mrsec.wisc.edu/forces-of-fluids-density-and-buoyancy/) · [Clemson torque lab](https://science.clemson.edu/physics/labs/labs/207/torque/index.html)
2. **Temperature and effervescence is the strongest chemistry experiment using the same release.** Students control a variable, collect a numerical duration, and can evaluate replicate variation. It needs identical tablets, stable temperatures and a clearly defined end-of-fizz rule. It drops out if approved temperature levels yield overlapping durations, a reliable endpoint cannot be recovered from the available view, the trial is too long for the session, or tablets stick, chip or splash on delivery. Bench observations can answer those questions before the arm is involved. [ACS Fizz Race](https://www.acs.org/education/celebrating-chemistry-editions/2021-ncw/fizz-race.html)
3. **Qualitative pH is the strongest way to keep the team’s chemistry question.** An unknown and a measured control give the student a reason to test, repeat, and interpret. The mounted strip route preserves the classification question and avoids tipping a filled vial. It gives a smaller visible signal, and needs an accessible strip or probe reading from the actual trial. The three-vial color-change route earns a physical slot if a validated fixed dose produces a clean and readable result. Physical pH drops out if controls fail, carryover or optics cause misclassification, the evidence is inaccessible, or the venue restricts the required reagents. Torque or fizz can preserve student agency while pH continues virtually. [ACS red cabbage procedure](https://www.acs.org/education/activities/red-cabbage-indicator.html) · [University of Maine strip procedure](https://extension.umaine.edu/4h/stem-toolkits/your-future-in-aquaculture-testing-the-waters/activity-5-ph/) · [Perkins accessible pH measurement](https://www.perkins.org/resource/acid-base-titration-using-nonvisual-tools/)

## 7. Practical validation tests

The thresholds below are proposed prototype gates. Set final tolerances from the actual apparatus, the learning objective, and the allotted time. Report attempts and failures by station, sample and phase. Use independent observation or measurement to confirm completion.

| Test | Method and measurements | Proposed pass or decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Identify the unit, map the working area and check stopping** | Record follower/servo labels, supply, mount and calibration. Dry-run the complete approach, release/dip and exit with a dummy at the actual intended stations. Measure the minimum clearance to fixture rims, cables and electronics. With a low-mass dummy inside containment, test stopping at approach, transport and release. Log post-stop movement, payload behavior and restart state. | Positive measured clearance with a margin for the observed loaded error. Safe stop behavior in all three phases and no uncommanded restart. Use supervised recovery for the intended failure conditions. |
| **Repeatability, grip and payload at task load** | Weigh the actual carrier and sample. At the near and far planned stations, repeat approaches from two previous positions, unloaded and at the heaviest planned task load. Start with 10 arrivals per critical loaded pose across two setups. Use a fixed target and ruler or scaled camera to log tip offset and spread in mm. Measure whether the grip slips during the full path and a 10-second hold at the most demanding orientation. Log faults, sag and any progressive drift. | Every tested loaded arrival stays inside the task capture region with clearance for the object and gripper. A practical starting margin is at least twice the largest measured relevant error where fixture size permits. Check the first and last arrivals as the arm warms. No slips, collisions, stalls or lost parts. Narrow the path or improve the fixture if needed. |
| **Shared release and buoyancy result** | Establish human-run controls first. Choose two known samples safely away from neutral buoyancy. A starting design target is measured average density at or below `0.8 ρ_water` and at or above `1.2 ρ_water`, with safe sample masses and ample clearance. Use a fixed side view. Randomize sample/condition order across 10 full arm trials, including both classes and at least two complete resets. Record contact, landed state, wall contacts, time to stable classification and reset time. | Correct physical outcome and correct text or spoken readback for every control/sample, with a stable outcome within a proposed five seconds of water contact. No lost samples, spills outside containment or gripper contact with water. If the physics works by hand and fails by arm, adjust release and tank geometry. |
| **Fizz result and endpoint repeatability** | Run at least five fresh human drops per temperature before arm trials. Record actual water temperature at contact, volume, tablet identity, and contact time from video. Pilot an endpoint such as the start of five consecutive seconds without newly rising visible bubbles, then keep the rule fixed. Have two observers time a subset independently. Run both temperatures again using the arm and compare with the hand trials. | For a clear short demo, seek nonoverlapping observed duration ranges in the small pilot, separation greater than twice the typical observer disagreement, and two runs that fit the demo window. Compare run-level observations before asserting a temperature effect. If the baseline fails, adjust approved conditions or choose another experiment. |
| **pH dip and reading** | Pilot three blinded reference categories with known standards or an independently calibrated meter. Run at least five trials per category with fresh strips, fixed light, locked camera settings if used, and maker-specified dip/read timing. Verify a neutral control following acidic and basic trials. Record unreadable strips, depth errors, readback disagreements, and carryover. | All pilot control categories agree with the reference and can be read without color alone. For a coarse strip scale, record bands and ambiguous results. Repeat with fresh unknowns only after the controls pass. If the strip and reader disagree, resolve with the independent measurement. |
| **Prepared-dose delivery, if pursued** | Start with water. Use a scale of suitable resolution under the receiver, zero it, and measure source loss and delivered mass for at least 10 cycles per dose/route. Weigh the catch tray or inspect for spills, record residue and drips, and include the return/reset. As an example, use a 10.0 g target and a scale resolving 0.1 g or better. Compare a stationary supported dispenser or cradle against a carried cup only if both can be tested safely. | A prototype example is 9.5–10.5 g delivered each cycle, zero external drips, correct destination and successful reset. A qualitative pH lesson can derive tolerance from the dose range that gives the correct control band. Quantitative dilution needs an error budget on the resulting concentration. Use mass as mass, or convert to volume with the liquid’s density at the test temperature. |
| **Dry torque fallback** | Zero the unloaded beam and measure each weight and pocket distance. Pick three known setups: left tilt, right tilt, and nominal balance. Run five loads per condition, remove any loading lock, and read beam position with the arm clear. | Both tilt directions are unambiguous, and the nominal balance consistently stays within a premarked level band after release. If friction masks the expected difference, increase the moment spacing or adjust the pivot before using it as a student test. |
| **End-to-end learning and access** | Ask a student or supervised pilot participant to choose a control and variable, predict, direct the action, identify the source of the observation, request a repeat if needed, and explain what the result supports. Try the alternative input and usable stop path. Include one failed/no-result trial and verify how the learner can tell. Time the complete run and staff reset. When possible, compare the same task with a directed assistant or adapted fixture and include intended users in follow-up. | A participant completes the decision and evidence loop and explains the result using the data. A failed transfer or reading appears as an incomplete trial. Log help, time, misreadings and task ownership. A pilot checks usability issues and does not establish accessibility across users. [Penn State accommodations](https://equity.psu.edu/offices/student-disability-resources/accommodations/implementation-guides/science-labs) · [NGSS investigation and analysis practices](https://www.nextgenscience.org/sites/ngss/files/Appendix%20F%20%20Science%20and%20Engineering%20Practices%20in%20the%20NGSS%20-%20FINAL%20060513.pdf) |

For the selected demo, rehearse the complete sequence in the final layout. A reasonable hackathon screening target is 30/30 correct end-to-end trials across the supported choices, including at least three rebuilds or restarts, with zero uncontained spills, collisions, wrong deliveries or false success reports. Include recovery behavior in separate intentional fault trials. Thirty clean independent trials would still allow an underlying failure probability of about 9.5% at a one-sided 95% bound. Shared fixtures and trial conditions may further limit that inference. Record the actual counts and representative failure modes when communicating readiness.

## 8. What this changes for OpenArms

1. **Move controlled buoyancy from fallback to first bench trial.** Use at least one matched comparison and a follow-up prediction. Try a dry tablet release next if the same fixture geometry works and manual timing data show a clear difference. A mandarin transfer can validate a move while the experiment tests whether that move enables a student to produce and interpret evidence. [Shared OpenArms analysis](https://chatgpt.com/share/6af80b60-1a80-83e9-84f2-90c34e524f89) · [UW–Madison practical](https://education.mrsec.wisc.edu/forces-of-fluids-density-and-buoyancy/) · [ACS Fizz Race](https://www.acs.org/education/celebrating-chemistry-editions/2021-ncw/fizz-race.html)
2. **Qualify fixed-station movement and release first, then qualify dipping or a fixture-delivered portion when the lesson requires it.** For qualitative pH, a dip and trustworthy reading can keep the original scientific question. If liquid dosing is tested, use delivered mass and completed reset as evidence. Compare the resulting workflow with an adapted instrument or directed assistance for the same objective. 3. **Use simulation first where it adds a scientifically testable choice.** Extend buoyancy by letting learners change fluid density, mass and volume independently. Use mass-balance dilution and validated acid–base models for variable volumes and titration. Use labeled reference time series for biological and fizz experiments until there is evidence for predictive kinetics. Report live observations, recorded data, and model results with their provenance.
