# What should the OpenArms simulation help students learn?

HackPrinceton research handoff · November 14, 2026

**Prioritize one student-directed investigation that connects a model to a real observation.** The simulation can let students vary a condition the SO-101 cannot reliably reproduce, see its predicted effect, and use a physical measurement to judge where the model applies. For the hackathon, that means one scientific question, one student-chosen physical trial, and one additional modeled condition. If the team can measure concentration or a defensible proxy, dilution is the cleanest quantitative candidate. If the hardware can only demonstrate an indicator response, use a qualitative comparison at the resolution the observation supports.

The [shared OpenArms analysis](https://chatgpt.com/share/6af80b60-1a80-83e9-84f2-90c34e524f89) proposed preview first, virtual expansion second, and inquiry support third. It also distinguished the immediate hardware checkout from the educational demo. This report keeps student control as a working hypothesis and puts the inquiry first for the educational demo. A brief action confirmation can help a student correct an interpreted command. A simulated arm trajectory has a separate burden of proof.

This is a targeted review of original studies, research syntheses, official platform documentation, and teaching resources. Platform entries describe documented activities. Assistive-technology flows, private course features, and hardware performance were outside this desk review. Product documentation without a publication date is linked by title.

## 2. Educational evidence

| Finding | What the evidence supports for OpenArms |
| --- | --- |
| **Guided inquiry improves learning across studied settings.** Lazonder and Harmsen’s meta-analysis of 72 studies reported a positive effect of guidance on learning outcomes (standardized effect d = 0.50, 95% CI 0.37–0.62). | Let students choose a condition, state what they expect, and decide how to interpret the result. Prompt novices to name what they will change, keep constant and measure. The pooled estimate cannot identify the best prompt or number of choices for this lesson. [Lazonder and Harmsen, 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366) · [Chen and Klahr, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8624.00081) |
| **Average conceptual learning is similar across physical and virtual inquiry in the available comparisons.** A 35-study meta-analysis found no statistically significant overall advantage (Hedges’ g = −0.14, 95% CI −0.33 to 0.06). Twenty-three studies were in physics and few measured inquiry skills separately. A 147-student pendulum study found no conceptual advantage for simulation over physical manipulation or for slow-motion over real-time replay. Experimentation strategy mattered. | Select the medium based on the goal. For explaining an ideal relation, a simple simulation can be sufficient. Instruction should reveal whether students can design a fair comparison or reason about data. Faster feedback and clearer graphics alone are insufficient evidence of that learning. [Muilwijk and Lazonder, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1163024) · [Pendulum study, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2012.08.006) |
| **Blending can improve conceptual learning when each activity contributes something.** In a study of 70 undergraduates on light and color, a blend designed around complementary affordances outperformed either medium alone on conceptual tests. An experiment with 50 elementary students found benefits to using simulation alongside real circuits in that lesson. Extra guidance slowed inquiry in the combined condition without much additional gain. | Pair the modes where the virtual run isolates a variable and the physical run supplies an observation worth interpreting. Both results are from specific lessons. They leave one-off chemistry demos, robotic mediation and the best sequence untested. [Olympiou and Zacharia, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.20463) · [Jaakkola, Nurmi and Veermans, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.20386) |
| **Students can learn to use discrepancies as evidence.** Introductory physics students who repeatedly compared datasets and models and acted on those comparisons were more likely than a course comparison group to propose method improvements and explain model limitations after prompts were faded. | Ask what an observed discrepancy would justify checking or changing. Give feedback on the fairness and interpretation of the test while leaving the explanation to the student. The study involved repeated practice across a course. A single demo can show the reasoning opportunity. [Holmes, Wieman and Bonn, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26283351/) |
| **Virtual preparation has some direct evidence, with a narrow scope.** In a randomized study of 189 undergraduates, a virtual microbiology preparation exercise and a face-to-face demonstration produced no statistically significant difference in subsequent physical lab scores. | Rehearsing a procedure is plausible. The study neither tested approval of a robot action nor estimated whether a 3D preview improves a student’s ability to spot a mislabeled vessel. Those are separate tests. [Makransky, Thisgaard and Gadegaard, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0155895) |

**Inclusive interaction is part of collecting and reasoning from evidence.** A student needs equivalent access to the choice of condition, the action about to run, the state after execution, the readings and their units, and the original prediction. Smith and colleagues describe the difficulty of communicating changing state and feedback in a PhET prototype for blind learners. WCAG 2.2 provides criteria for keyboard access, alternatives to dragging, use of color and status messages. These are design and evaluation references. Learning or access improvements for OpenArms require observation with students using their own input methods. [Smith, Lewis and Moore, 2017](https://openresearch.ocadu.ca/id/eprint/1576/) · [W3C WCAG 2.2, first published 2023](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)

## 3. Simulation capability matrix

| Capability | Educational purpose | Accessibility benefit | Evidence and hackathon priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Choose a test and record a prediction before seeing results** | Elicit a directional or categorical claim. Keep at least two defensible choices and show what stays fixed. | Labeled choices and editable text allow the same decision through a non-voice path. | **Core.** Guidance has broad support. The proposed prompt is a lesson-design inference. [Guided inquiry meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366) |
| **Inspect, edit and confirm the planned physical action** | Let the student verify that the selected sample, destination and portion answer their question. | Concise text such as “Add one prepared water portion to Sample A,” with equivalent spoken output when available, makes the intent reviewable without animation. | **Core when executing live.** A control and comprehension requirement. Benefit over a direct command needs testing. [PhET description research](https://openresearch.ocadu.ca/id/eprint/1576/) |
| **Record the selected condition and observation in a shared trial table** | Preserve the evidence needed to compare like quantities, including units, how the value was obtained and incomplete runs. | Readable rows with explicit labels give students an alternative to interpreting motion, color or a small camera display. | **Core.** Data-to-model comparison has empirical support. Choice of interaction and labels needs user testing. [Holmes et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26283351/) |
| **Run a simple scientific model at the matched setting and one extra setting** | Provide an explicit prediction for comparison and let students explore an otherwise unavailable condition. | A labeled input and text result support repeatable, self-paced trials with the same task in each mode. | **Core for the proposed simulation demo, conditional on a defensible model or measured reference.** Breadth beyond one variable can wait. [Physical and virtual inquiry meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1163024) |
| **Explain the result and propose a follow-up** | Ask whether the evidence supports the prediction and what assumption or procedure deserves a check. | Keep predictions and readings available while the student writes, selects, or dictates a response as supported. | **Core.** One claim, evidence and next-check prompt is enough for the demo. [Holmes et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26283351/) · [Go-Lab inquiry scaffolds](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40561-014-0003-6) |
| **Animate the actual proposed path or reproduce a full lab in 3D** | Potential orientation benefit when spatial relationships are themselves relevant to the decision. | Optional spatial cues can complement text. Successful completion should also be possible using the action summary and observations. | **Defer.** Test added error detection or spatial understanding first. An immersive lab study found higher presence alongside lower learning in its headset condition. That result has limited relevance to a brief optional preview. [Makransky, Terkildsen and Mayer, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.12.007) |

## 4. Physical versus virtual and where integration adds value

| Activity | Best fit and integration pattern |
| --- | --- |
| **Isolate one variable or explore more conditions** | Virtual trials can vary dilution ratios or block densities while holding everything else fixed. A second setting offers a transfer question or shows a boundary condition that the hardware cannot conveniently reach. The case for adding trials is strongest when students use them to revise a prediction or test a rule. Broad learning evidence supports virtual conceptual inquiry. The added effect of this particular extension is unknown. [Muilwijk and Lazonder, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1163024) |
| **Review an action before execution** | Review the substantive changes to the experiment, including which vessel, where, and how much. A visual route might help with spatial orientation if it represents the current setup and the learner needs that information. York’s virtual-to-remote course demonstrates feasibility and perceived preparation. It does not isolate the effect of preview. Georgia Tech’s Robotarium provides a separate example of simulation and dedicated physical safety methods for actual robots. [York case](https://www.quanser.com/case-study/york-university/) · [Robotarium original report, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04730) · [Robotarium safety research, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00640) |
| **Confront data from real materials** | Take a physical reading when the goal is to judge a noisy or limited observation, recognize a failed transfer, or decide whether to repeat. Pair a model value with an observation of the *same quantity* at a shared setting. Label whether evidence is live, previously recorded, or modeled, and retain uncertainty or measurement resolution where known. Authentic recorded data can serve evidence interpretation when a live trial is unavailable. Only a live trial tests whether the student’s current choice caused a physical action. [Holmes et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26283351/) · [LabsLand documented access modes](https://labsland.com/licenses/?lang=en) |
| **Practice manual technique** | Direct handling matters if students are assessed on pipette feel, glassware assembly, or responding manually to apparatus. Robot control can let students direct a procedure and interpret outcomes. Use a separate assessment for hands-on technique. [ACS laboratory curriculum guidance](https://www.acs.org/education/policies/acs-approval-program/guidelines/laboratory.html) |

VISIR shows why the source of evidence matters. Students configure a circuit in a virtual-looking workbench, and a real switching matrix and instruments return the measurements. Available components and connections constrain the questions students can test. A virtual-looking control panel can represent physical execution without predicting a scientific outcome. [VISIR user manual](https://labsland.com/pub/docs/experiments/VISIR/labsland_user_Manual_VISIR_en.pdf) · [VISIR limitations study, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2813-8856/2/4/20)

A matching arm graphic can still omit moved vessels, grip slip, contact, a partial pour and sensor drift. Treat the preview as a statement of intent. Physical execution needs independently verified limits and supervision appropriate to the equipment. Use the actual run status and measured observation as evidence. Calling the preview a digital twin would overstate what the hackathon can demonstrate.

## 5. Scientific modeling opportunities

| Candidate and learning question | Defensible model or dataset | Limits and evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Conservative dilution. What happens to concentration when water is added?** | Solute conservation gives C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ and C₂/C₁ = V₁/V₂. Two equal volumes, one stock and one water, give 0.5× stock concentration under the model. Use actual final volume if the additive approximation is inadequate. A measured absorbance ratio can estimate relative concentration via A = εℓc when the dye and instrument are in a verified linear range. | Assume mixing, conserved solute and known starting and final volumes. For absorbance, check blank, wavelength, path length and linearity against standards. Human color judgments or raw camera RGB alone cannot support a numerical concentration. A qualitative observation can support a directional claim if the tested colors are distinguishable and the method is disclosed. [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Molarity, 2019](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/3-3-molarity) · [UC Davis spectrophotometry lab manual, 2018](https://chemistry.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk196/files/inline-files/Chem%202A%20Lab%20Manual%202018%20Summer.pdf) |
| **Indicator and pH. Can a reference indicator distinguish acidic and basic samples?** | For a selected indicator, use its published transition interval, or collect a chart of observations from known reference buffers with the actual indicator formulation and viewing setup. A cabbage extract chart can support category-level comparison. Use a calibrated pH meter as a separate reading if the task requires numeric pH. | pH is −log₁₀ of hydrogen ion activity. Concentration is an approximation for dilute solutions under appropriate conditions. For a dilute monoprotic strong acid far from neutral, an ideal 10× dilution raises pH by about one unit. Near neutral water autoionization matters. Weak acids and buffers need equilibrium assumptions and known composition. Cabbage extract contains several pigments, and extract preparation, sample color and lighting can confound the observation. Avoid a universal color-to-pH conversion for household liquids. [ACS red-cabbage activity](https://www.acs.org/education/activities/red-cabbage-indicator.html) · [OpenStax pH](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-2-ph-and-poh) · [OpenStax indicator ranges](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-7-acid-base-titrations) · [IUPAC pH recommendations, 2002](https://publications.iupac.org/pac/74/11/2169/index.html) |
| **Buoyancy. Does a higher mass always mean sinking?** | Use average density ρ = m/V and Archimedes’ principle Fᵦ = ρfluid g Vdisplaced. For a freely floating object at equilibrium, Vsubmerged/Vobject = ρobject/ρfluid. Compare two equal-mass blocks of different volume. A real float/sink observation and a virtual float/sink result make a comparable categorical pair. | Assume a still uniform fluid, free movement, and an intact object of known mass and exterior volume. Contact, trapped air, water uptake and surface tension can change observations. If measuring apparent weight, keep the object clear of the container and document tare and resolution. [OpenStax College Physics 2e, Archimedes’ Principle](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/11-7-archimedes-principle) · [PhET Buoyancy: Basics activity view](https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/buoyancy-basics/1.2.0/buoyancy-basics_a11y_view.html) |
| **Boyle’s law. Which relationship fits pressure and volume?** | At fixed gas quantity and temperature in the ideal approximation, P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. Predict a pressure ratio or graph absolute pressure against 1/V. Use documented measured syringe traces as a potential evidence source if usable values and provenance are available. | Allow temperature to settle, account for tubing and sensor dead volume, and respect the measurement range. This is a benchmark exercise for the simulation decision. Physical suitability for OpenArms is open. [OpenStax ideal gas law](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/9-2-relating-pressure-volume-amount-and-temperature-the-ideal-gas-law) · [LabsLand Boyle lesson and instrument limits](https://teach.labsland.com/en/lessons/boyles-law-pressure-volume-inquiry/) |

Before showing a claim, verify the expected result against a textbook calculation, worked limit, or reference measurement at the intended settings. Compare a small number of physical calibration or repeat runs where available and name large discrepancies or unknowns. A reference dataset needs identifiable conditions, method, units and source. Select only recorded conditions represented in that dataset. Generate model outcomes from the stated equations or bounds, and keep student predictions distinct from both model outputs and instrument readings.

## 6. Differentiation and counterevidence

- **Virtual chemistry and guided inquiry already exist.** PhET and ChemCollective cover the central concepts at greater breadth. Labster documents Accessibility Mode for the example pH module. Go-Lab already supports the inquiry sequence. If students only need to understand how dilution changes concentration, the robot adds time and equipment dependencies to an activity existing tools can serve. [PhET pH Scale](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/ph-scale-basics) · [ChemCollective dilution](https://chemcollective.org/activities/vlab/3) · [Go-Lab federation](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40561-014-0003-6)
- **Real data and paired physical/virtual modes already exist.** OpenSTEM approaches this in wet chemistry. LabsLand supplies real recorded data with inquiry tasks. York pairs virtual rehearsal and real remote operation. Visibility of the student's exact causal choice is a narrower claim than any first-to-market claim. [OpenSTEM](https://stem.open.ac.uk/study/openstem-labs/openscience-laboratory) · [LabsLand Boyle](https://teach.labsland.com/en/lessons/boyles-law-pressure-volume-inquiry/) · [York case](https://www.quanser.com/case-study/york-university/)
- **The plausible value is a student-controlled comparison in a lab that already matters to the course.** A learner picks a condition, verifies a physical manipulation they otherwise need help with, receives an accessible record of the actual result, and can test the same scientific idea at further settings. The advantage over an accessible simulator paired with a student-directed assistant or adapted instrument remains a hypothesis. If the robot offers only one meaningful physical choice, if the result is available exclusively as live video, or if the comparison cannot change the student's explanation, the integration claim is weak. [OpenArms shared analysis](https://chatgpt.com/share/6af80b60-1a80-83e9-84f2-90c34e524f89)

## 7. Recommended simulation MVP

**Demonstrate one question with two real choices and a comparison the student can explain.** Use the experiment the hardware team can substantiate. Dilution is the working example for this research handoff. The sequence below assumes two supported physical conditions and a usable reading, with one condition selected for live execution.

1. **Predict.** Ask how the concentration changes when a specified extra portion of water is added. Let the student choose a physical condition and record an expected direction or ratio, as supported by the measurement.
2. **Check and run.** Show the selected sample, destination and portion in a short action summary. Let the student correct or approve it with a non-voice control path. Record one live result, with the session's actual observation and whether execution succeeded.
3. **Compare and explain.** Reveal the modeled result at the matching condition, then let the student try one further setting virtually. Keep the student's prediction, the model result and the measured observation individually labeled. Ask for one evidence-backed explanation and one possible follow-up check. Match the model and measurement in the same units or in the same defensible categories.

For a color-only pH demo, compare a documented or calibrated reference category with the actual observation, and label the reference by its origin. Use one additional in-range reference case as a virtual comparison if the data support it. If the setup cannot produce a comparable observation, use a clearly labeled recorded comparison for the educational exercise and present the live action as a separate hardware demonstration. Keep the record of a missing measurement visible. Defer multi-experiment breadth, full trajectory visualization and digital-twin claims.

The demo succeeds educationally if a learner can choose a condition, correct an understood action, identify which result came from the physical trial, and use a specific observation to support or revise their prediction. A small pilot can observe those behaviors. A learning-gain claim requires an assessment and comparison beyond this demonstration.

## 8. Open research questions

| Question | Smallest useful validation |
| --- | --- |
| Does the paired activity improve reasoning beyond a good virtual activity with the same prompts? | Give learners either the paired activity or the same model and prompts alone, followed by a new case. Score control of variables, use of evidence and explanation of a model limitation. Treat a small sample as exploratory. |
| Does the action summary let students catch an interpretation error, and does animation add anything? | Seed a harmless wrong sample or destination in a rehearsal. Compare text alone with text plus visualization. Observe detection and recovery, including through the intended non-voice input. |
| Do students experience meaningful control over the science? | Observe whether students can choose a condition, revise their choice and explain why it matters. Ask what decision they made that the system could have made for them. |
| Can students complete the comparison with equivalent evidence access? | Have target users try the complete flow with their input method. Check selection, confirmation, measurement, comparison and response. Record where they need another person to decode an observation or take over a decision. |
| Is a discrepancy informative at the resolution of the equipment? | Ask the experiment team for repeated readings, actual portion sizes or sample properties and measurement resolution. Check whether an instructor can explain the largest difference. Simplify the outcome if the available data only support a category or direction. |
| Is the paired exercise worth the apparatus time for instructors? | Ask instructors to grade the same student explanation from a matched virtual-only activity and the paired activity. Identify an objective that benefits from a real observation, and test whether recorded data or an accessible physical alternative could fulfill it. |

## 9. What this changes for OpenArms

1. **Put the inquiry first.** Make the student's variable choice, prediction, observation and explanation the core demo. Give action confirmation the narrow job of letting the student verify what will happen. [Lazonder and Harmsen, 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366)
2. **Choose the experiment by what students can compare.** Use one model and one real observation at a shared condition, with one extra virtual test. Require comparable units or categories, explicit provenance and a non-voice path through the task. [Holmes et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26283351/) · [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
3. **Test the value added by the physical trial.** Compare the paired lesson with the same inquiry prompts in a virtual-only activity. Look for a better explanation or an independently directed decision that the physical trial uniquely allowed. [Muilwijk and Lazonder, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1163024) · [OpenSTEM practicals](https://stem.open.ac.uk/study/openstem-labs/openscience-laboratory)
