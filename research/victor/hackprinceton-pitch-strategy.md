# How should OpenArms pitch at HackPrinceton?

Fall 2026 judging prep · November 15, 2026 · Working plan for Victor and the team

**Enter Education. Lead with the student directing a real experiment, then show how the AI handles unfamiliar work and makes a repeat faster.** Use one supervised two-vessel robot experiment, followed by clearly labeled simulated evidence. The physical bench shows why student control matters. The simulated traces let judges see what the agent perceived, decided, verified and saved.

## The story

> OpenArms is a voice-controlled lab assistant that helps students with limited hand or arm mobility direct science experiments. When an action is unfamiliar, an AI expert looks at the setup and assembles a plan from supported robot actions. After a skill passes checks, OpenArms saves it for that setup so the student can ask for the same action again in their own words.

Put the student in charge of a real decision. They ask for an action, hear or read what the system understood, confirm or correct it, and ask what happened. A robot-compatible station handles an approved manipulation. The student hears the observation and decides what to do next. Keep visible status and direct STOP available. Explain that teaching staff prepare and supervise the bench. For Education, make the learning question audible. If the indicator trial qualifies, ask what happens to the prepared tea when the acid sample is added. The student directs the pour, describes the actual observation and chooses a comparison such as adding water in a modeled or planned trial. Label the comparison according to what the team actually ran. The first user is a student who can direct the experiment and needs help with a specific reach, grip or pour. Student usability and course adoption remain questions for a pilot. ### The AI decision judges should remember

- **First encounter.** Gemini Live handles the conversation while a separate expert uses current scene evidence and supported tools to resolve an unfamiliar request. It proposes an exact action the student can review. A bounded executor handles motion after authorization. A separate camera or simulator observer checks what happened.
- **Same objective, new wording.** The evaluator can admit a parameterized, versioned skill for a matching setup. An equivalent request can reuse it and skip the expert round trip while keeping scene checks, consent and outcome verification. Show the skill name or version and provider call count so the audience can see what was reused.
- **Changed conditions.** Current evidence and setup checks still govern the action. Show a changed virtual layout with a verified fresh scene decision if the run is ready. A supported adjustment or a clear refusal of an invalid binding both make the system's response visible. A saved skill is scoped to a validated setup. Claims about spatial adaptation require their own run.
- **Separate improvement evidence.** The measured first-use versus repeat result is skill reuse (D3). Claim D4 improvement only for a candidate with a real recorded limitation, an unchanged baseline and comparable evaluation that shows what improved. The stored skill changes the application's behavior through retrieval and validation. Model weights remain fixed.

Devpost permits multiple sponsor opt-ins alongside the single main track. It also says track winners are ineligible for Best Overall. The pitch should work for all judges without multiple scripts. For **Creativity**, show an AI skill becoming reusable. For **Utility**, show a completed student action and receipt. For **Charity**, show the specific barrier and control the student gains. For **Avidity**, have the team explain one engineering decision they made and the next pilot they want to run.
